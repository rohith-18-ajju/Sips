What these mean

{% if a.kind == "stock" %}

P/E is the share price divided by yearly profit per share. A high P/E means investors pay more for each rupee of profit. P/B compares the price to the company's net worth. EPS is the profit earned for each share.

ROE shows how well the company turns shareholders' money into profit. Debt to equity shows how much it borrows compared with its own money, and is not very meaningful for banks. Dividend yield is the yearly payout as a percentage of the price. Beta above 1 means the stock tends to move more than the market.

{% else %}

NAV is the price of one fund unit. Returns per year show the steady yearly growth over that period. Direct plan growth versions are shown, which usually have lower fees than regular plans.

{% endif %}

Volatility measures how much the price moves up and down in a year. Biggest fall is the largest drop from a peak in the last 3 years. Past performance does not guarantee future returns. This is an educational guide, not advice to buy or sell.
